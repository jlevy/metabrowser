exp-100 protocol: a held first screen's Markdown asked for all at once (H120, bead mb-g5wf)
Registered 2026-11-17, before the first timed run.

Question. exp-099 found that a pull request page opened from a warm cache holds its
title, out of sight, until every text on its first screen has its rendered Markdown, and
that those texts were asked for MARKDOWN_CONCURRENCY (2) at a time. On #487 (four texts)
the title came 212 to 258 ms after the record's arrival, the four Markdown requests
starting 7, 30, 73 and 102 ms after it. Does asking for the held texts at once shorten
that wait?

Decision rule (rough cut, 1.3x, because other sessions keep the load at 5 to 20):
  Deciding: pr-warm title_after_pull (the title's first frame minus the first GET pull's
  response end). Accepted when the candidate/control pair ratio is under 1/1.3 in at
  least 3 of 5 pairs and the candidate median is lower; rejected when it is under 1/1.3
  in at most 1 pair and the medians do not differ by 1.3x; otherwise unresolved.
  Mechanism check, not deciding: pr-warm markdown_after_pull lower, markdown_requests
  unchanged (4).
  Guards (each fails when beyond 1.3x in 3 or more of 5 pairs): pr-warm and
  pr-warm-reload title, description_final and complete; pr-fresh and pr-store title and
  description_final; pr-warm pull_ms.
  Reported, not judged: pr-warm-reload title_after_pull (a warm server renders in 3 to
  11 ms, so the saving there is under the rough cut's resolution).

Budget. Stop before a round when GitHub's core budget is under 1500 or the disk under
2 GB (the driver's defaults).

Limits declared in advance. One pull request with four first-screen texts; a page whose
first screen holds one or two texts gains nothing. A fresh server per run makes each
first Markdown render cost 50 to 80 ms; a long-running server's is the reload step.
Five pairs resolve a large effect only.
