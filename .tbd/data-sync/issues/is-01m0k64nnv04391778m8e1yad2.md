Phase 1 of the spec: the source document, with nothing on screen changing.

Convert recommended-file-types.toml to recommended-file-types.yml and add the two fields that make it the whole declaration: `linguist`, naming the upstream language a family corresponds to or null, and `color`, carrying the upstream hex as `source` beside the oklch hue we actually paint.

The conversion is 954 lines of mechanical edit, so it is verified by equality rather than by reading: load the TOML and the YAML and assert the resolved registries are identical apart from the new fields. That is the only check that makes an edit this size trustworthy.

Per the format's own version model, changing the definition-file structure requires a new registry schema version: schema_version 2, registry_revision 3. The loader, the schemas, the generator, the conformance corpus, and the format document's "Recommended File-Type Definitions" section all move together, and the TOML is deleted rather than left as a second source.

Nothing visible changes in this phase, which is the point: a regression in phase 2 is then unambiguous.

## Notes

Phase 1 of the spec: every family declares its hue, and lightness and chroma come from one rule.

The source stays TOML. An earlier draft proposed YAML to mirror linguist's own languages.yml; TOML is what the loader, schemas, generator, conformance corpus, and format documentation already speak, it is first-class in the Rust tooling this project may move to, and the gap was never the file format — colour was simply not in the file.

Two fields per family: `linguist`, naming the upstream language or omitted, and `hue`, an oklch hue. Nothing else about colour is declared, because lightness and chroma are derived.

The derivation is the substance. For a given hue, sRGB's most saturated point sits at a particular lightness, and both that lightness and the achievable chroma move with hue — which is why the current ramp runs from L 0.569 for blue to L 0.729 for yellow and looks arbitrary until you measure it. Against the most chroma sRGB holds at each slot's lightness and hue, the existing categories sit at 81% to 97% of maximum. The ramp is already "as vivid as the gamut allows near the hue's peak"; it has just never been written down.

So: find the hue's cusp, take chroma as a fixed fraction of the peak, take lightness as the cusp lightness pulled toward a mid target. Two constants, applied to all 56 families alike, measured and recorded beside the ramp.

38 hues convert from linguist. 18 families are not languages and need their own, and JSON joins them — #292929 is pure grey, chroma 0.000, so there is no hue to borrow.

Deviations are expected and recorded. Python converts to 246.5 degrees and TypeScript to 253.3, 6.8 apart: borrowing faithfully would make two of the most commonly co-occurring families indistinguishable, which is the defect this work exists to remove. The better-known family keeps its hue, the other moves the smallest amount that clears the distinctness floor, and the reason sits beside the declaration.
